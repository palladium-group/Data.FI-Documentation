Implementation governance connects national leadership, product decisions, and technical delivery. The structure must resolve clinical, program, data, architecture, security, operational, and funding questions. Delivery teams should not have to make policy decisions by default.

| Level | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| National oversight | Strategic alignment, policy decisions, major investment, data governance, and institutional ownership |
| Product governance | Product vision, priorities, scope and release approval, and cross-program needs |
| Technical governance | Architecture, standards, terminology, interfaces, security patterns, environments, and assurance evidence |
| Delivery team | Configure, develop, integrate, test, document, deploy, and resolve implementation issues |
| Operations and support | Environments, monitoring, user support, incidents, and continuity |
| User and community voice | Workflow, usability, accessibility, service quality, and implementation experience |

Confirm the [readiness questions](./getting-started.md#readiness-questions) before configuration or integration work starts.

## Content ownership

| Owner | Owns |
|---|---|
| Program and clinical leads | Service workflows, content, decision rules, and service-quality measures |
| Product owner | Product vision, priorities, backlog, release scope, and acceptance |
| Architecture and standards leads | Technical design, FHIR conformance, terminology patterns, and architecture decisions |
| Interoperability and data leads | Interfaces, mapping, identity, exchange quality, warehouse models, and indicators |
| Security and privacy leads | Risk assessment, access principles, controls, review, and incident requirements |
| Implementation and operations leads | Readiness, training, rollout, support, monitoring, continuity, and handover |

Record decisions in a decision log: options, rationale, authority, affected assets, and review date. See [Templates and assets](./templates.md).
